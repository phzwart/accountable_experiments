# Abstract (draft 2)

*Working title: Accountable experiments: autonomy under contract*

Autonomous experiments decide where to measure, and every site they leave out enters the result as a prediction. An accountable experiment certifies every action: a site is skipped because a stated claim covers it, with a checkable bound on the share of sites skipped wrongly, or measured because it was needed when the decision was made. The campaign runs under a contract fixed before it starts: the claims that justify a skip, a tolerance, a budget and a confidence. A skip is made under one of two claims: the site holds nothing of interest, or its signal, or a quantity derived from it, can be predicted within tolerance from coarser measurements. The levels of a design are fixed before the campaign, and a uniform calibration draw from each level, taken after the model is frozen, bounds the share of the level skipped under a false claim, whatever the model. On three fully measured datasets, an infrared map, a NaCl microdiffraction scan and a synthetic scattering phase map, observed exceedances matched those the bound allows. On the NaCl scan, a campaign guided by Gaussian-process uncertainty matched the certified campaigns in cost and error without stating its error rate; its 95 % intervals implied 5 % of predictable skips wrong, and 29 % were. Certifying the same model cost about 100 more measurements. The model set the cost of a guarantee: the infrared map needed 295 of 959 measurements with informed scores and 716 with random ones, and every decision can be replayed from the record.
