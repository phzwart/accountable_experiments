# Abstract (draft 2)

*Working title: Accountable experiments: autonomy under contract*

Autonomous experiments decide where to measure, and every site they leave out enters the result as a prediction. An accountable experiment certifies every action, the decision on each level of which sites to measure and which to skip under a stated claim. The campaign runs under a contract fixed before it starts: a plan of sites at the finest resolution sought, the claims that justify a skip, a tolerance, an error budget and a confidence. A skip is made under one of two claims: the site holds nothing of interest, or its signal, or a quantity derived from it, can be predicted within tolerance from coarser measurements. Each level of the plan is set before its calibration draw, and a uniform draw from the level, taken after the model is frozen, bounds at the stated confidence the share of the level's sites skipped under a false claim, whatever the model. The statistics are standard; the contribution is posing the experiment so that they apply inside a closed loop. On three fully measured datasets, an infrared map, a NaCl microdiffraction scan and a synthetic scattering phase map, levels exceeded their budget no more often than the stated confidence permits; plans certified for all levels together kept their budget on every level in at least 80 % of campaigns, at 12 % to 38 % more measurements than plans certified level by level. A Gaussian process trusted on its own uncertainty stated a rate that was right in one setting and three to ten times too low in others. The model and its scores set only the cost of a guarantee, and every decision can be replayed from the record.
