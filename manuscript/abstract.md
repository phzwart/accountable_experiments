# Abstract (draft 4)

*Working title: Accountable experiments: autonomy under contract*

An autonomous experiment decides where to measure and where not to, and a skipped site seldom can be revisited once sample, beam time or reagent is spent. Each skip therefore needs a bound on how often skips are wrong, fixed in advance. The model steering the campaign cannot supply that bound. It selects the sites on which its own error is then judged, a circular analysis, and a coherent Bayesian forecaster assigns probability one to its own calibration, so its stated error rate confirms itself. We show one way to break this cycle. A contract fixed before the campaign names a plan of sites, the claims under which a site may be skipped, and a budget on wrong skips held with a stated confidence. In each round a model is fitted and frozen, a uniform calibration sample is measured, and conformal thresholds set from its labels decide the skips. The bound is probably approximately correct (PAC): the share of a round skipped wrongly stays within the budget in at least the stated fraction of campaigns, whatever the model. On a fully measured NaCl microdiffraction scan, with one frozen Gaussian process and calibration sample shared by both cuts, conformal thresholds kept the stated rate in 78 % to 89 % of 80 campaigns, against a target of 80 %, with each of three kernels and with a predictor guessing at random. Cut by its own uncertainty, the process kept its rate in 69 % to 100 % with a noise term and in none with a kernel that assumed a smooth map; with that kernel the thresholds measured 723 sites against 317, and the map refitted from them was better in each of 20 designs. Levels kept their budget at the stated confidence on an infrared map and a synthetic scattering map as well. A held-out audit gives the finished map its own bound, as the free R value does for a crystal structure.
