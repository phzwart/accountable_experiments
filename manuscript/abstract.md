# Abstract (draft 2)

*Working title: Accountable experiments: autonomy under contract*

Autonomous experiments decide where to measure, and every site they leave out enters the result as a prediction. An accountable experiment certifies every action: a site is skipped because a stated claim covers it, with a checkable bound on the share of sites skipped wrongly, or measured because it was needed when the decision was made. The campaign runs under a contract fixed before it starts: the claims that justify a skip, a tolerance, a budget and a confidence. A skip is made under one of two claims: the site holds nothing of interest, or its signal, or a quantity derived from it, can be predicted within tolerance from coarser measurements. The levels of a design are fixed before the campaign, and a uniform calibration draw from each level, taken after the model is frozen, bounds the share of the level skipped under a false claim, whatever the model. On three fully measured datasets, an infrared map, a NaCl microdiffraction scan and a synthetic scattering phase map, observed exceedances matched those the bound allows. Left uncertified, the same Gaussian process misstated its share of wrong skips in either direction, by up to two orders of magnitude on the synthetic map, and an operating point chosen on its statement cost up to 158 times the cheapest option; certified campaigns never paid more on average than they expected. The model set the cost of a guarantee: the infrared map needed 295 of 959 measurements with informed scores and 716 with random ones, and every decision can be replayed from the record.
