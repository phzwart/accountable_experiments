# 6 Discussion (draft 3)

## The price of knowing in advance

A bound is worth most when it is known at the moment a decision rests on it. Two simpler routes give a statement about a campaign: a uniform sample of the same size, or a model steering on its own uncertainty, each followed by an audit of the finished map. Both report once every skip has been made, and a skip found wrong then is a site that can rarely be measured again. The contract moves the statement to the decision, and it charges for the move in measurements. Calibration costs 40 sites on each certified level, up to a sixth of the IR map, a share that falls as maps grow, since the budget and the confidence alone set the sample size. A plan certified for all levels together cost 13 % to 41 % more than one certified level by level (Section 5.1). On the NaCl scan the Gaussian process steering on its own statement stopped at 467 measurements and met its budget, which the full scan showed afterwards; the same process inside the contract measured 761 and knew its bound before each level was decided (Section 5.6). The price stays with the measurements: the final maps of the certified plans left no more sites outside tolerance than a uniform sample of the same size, and on the synthetic map a third as many (Section 5.4). The record adds a second value in failure. A level over budget is either chance at the stated rate or a sign that a condition of Section 3.1 failed, through drift, beam damage or a draw that was not uniform; seed sites measured again after each level expose drift (Section 5.6), and the contract states in advance what a flag stops. An uncertified agent leaves its errors unapportioned between model, data and chance.

## Any model, and this Gaussian process

The bound concerns a decision, and it holds whatever model proposes the skips. A model steering a campaign selects the sites on which it would be judged, so a check run on them is circular [Kriegeskorte2009]. A coherent Bayesian forecaster gives probability one to its own long-run calibration [Dawid1982], so the check has to come from outside the model. Intervals built by cross-validation, such as the jackknife+ [Barber2021], need the measured sites to be exchangeable with the skipped ones, and selection by the model breaks that unless the selection probabilities are known [Fannjiang2022; ZrnicCandes2024]; on the synthetic map rescaling to leave-one-out residuals shrank the stated uncertainty, because the measured sites were easier to predict than the skipped ones (Section 5.2). A better kernel may make a process honest on a given map, and whether it did is again known only afterwards; the contract makes the question moot for the guarantee and leaves it to the cost. A uniform sample drawn before the decision turns the check into a bound, through the order statistic of Section 3.3, at no cost beyond the calibration sample of the plan. Any kernel then enters as predictor and score, and the quality of its predictions returns as fewer measurements: under the conformal thresholds of Fig. 1, the processes measured 580 to 723 of about 1075 sites and the stock-picking chicken 891, all at the stated rate (Sections 5.2, 5.4). Ranking by the process's own probability of a wrong skip saved none over its positional uncertainty (Section 5.4). The process used here serves as an illustration, and what it did on these maps concerns this process alone.

## Scope of the guarantee

A map built under a guarantee serves its reader to the extent that the reader knows what the bound covers. The bound concerns the share of wrong skips among all design sites of a level, with probability over the calibration draw. Section 5.5 and SI 6 show the two consequences: wrong skips gather in space, and when signal is rare a budget met on every level can still cost a large share of the sites of interest. A label-conditional statement bounds the share of interesting sites lost (Section 5.5); it rests on the calibration sites of interest alone, so a rare signal calls for a larger draw. Labels compare with one measurement (Section 3.5). On the NaCl scan the width of the line varies across the sample at the scale of the tolerance, so about half of all predictions fail whatever the model, while counting noise moves the width far less (SI 2); the variation is structure in the sample, from strain, crystallite size or orientation, and measuring most sites is the correct outcome for a claim at that tolerance. Fully measured data check the rule against a fixed truth, and the 250 campaigns per configuration check its implementation. A live campaign remains untested: simulated drift broke the bound and moved most of the error into the measurements, where repeated seed sites found it (Section 5.6), and a drift so found can be modelled from the repeated sites and removed, or the calibration sites weighted by age at a bounded loss of coverage [Barber2023]; a measurement that disturbs its neighbours remains a question for the instrument.

## What is certified shapes the campaign

A guarantee is stated for one acceptance metric, and the metric deserves the same care as the budget. The bound holds for any metric fixed before the calibration draw, and the metric decides the rest: which sites fail prediction, where measurements are taken and where wrong skips gather (Section 5.5). The same holds for any autonomous experiment: the quantity a model predicts, and the error its acquisition anticipates, decide where measurements are taken [Noack2019; Noack2020]. The guarantee delivers the claim as specified, and features outside that claim carry no protection. The metric is therefore best fixed from the scientific question before beam time, written into the contract, and checked on a pilot scan for the cost it implies.

## Extensions

An audit sits over any campaign, whatever its rule, at the cost of its sample (Section 5.6). Certification of each level sits over any campaign that fixes its proposals for a level before its draw: the proposals of an acquisition-driven Gaussian process enter as a frozen score, and within an action the remaining sites can follow the shortest path of the instrument. Costs here are counted in sites; at an instrument each level adds a second pass, the calibration sample measured and reduced before the rest is decided, and a fast raster of the whole plan may compete with scattered single measurements. Any rate that counts sites can be bounded, and ratios such as the share of skips that are wrong are controlled through conformal p-values [Jin2023]. Two conditions carry every such statement: the calibration sites are an exchangeable draw from the population the statement concerns, a Mondrian category fixed before the draw taking the place of the level [Vovk2005], and the rule is non-anticipating (Section 3.1). For a bounded loss graded by the size of an error, confidence sequences for sampling without replacement [WaudbySmith2020] give a bound of the same kind and would let calibration stop as soon as the bound is settled. The design needs only a fixed plan, a claim that can be checked by measuring and a uniform draw, so it applies beyond the beamline, to autonomous synthesis, to campaigns of electronic-structure calculations and to agents that choose which computations to run. Optimisation is the natural next case; reweighting by the known selection mechanism [Tibshirani2019; Fannjiang2022; Barber2023], online control of a long-run rate [Gibbs2021] and level sets, each a mapping problem with a threshold fixed before its draw [CerouGuyader2007; Gotovos2013], offer routes to a bound.

## Accountable experiments

An accountable experiment fixes its contract before the first measurement and attaches a bound to every decision before the decision is taken. The workflow leaves both in its record. Each site carries its level, scores and reason; each level carries its calibration draw, picked failure probability, thresholds and bound. The guarantee rests on the order of operations, so the record suffices: predictor and scores are frozen and recorded before each draw, the draw is reproduced from its recorded random state, and every decision can be replayed, every bound recomputed (SI 5) and every measurement checked in hindsight (SI 8). Claims, tolerances and budgets form the contract and remain the experimenter's choice; the record shows that the contract was kept, and the audit reports, as the free R value does, how well the finished map holds.
