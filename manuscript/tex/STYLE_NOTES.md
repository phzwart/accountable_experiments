# Style notes from the author's edits

Collected from the comments and changes in the introduction (commit 119b09c, 23 Nov 2026). Apply to every section.

## Rules

1. **No negative examples.** Do not cite other work to show it failing or disputed (the autonomous-laboratory claim and its dispute were removed). Cite work for what it does.
2. **Rephrase for flow.** Prefer a sentence that leads into the next one ("For example, ...") over a list of clauses joined by semicolons and colons.
3. **Preview the result once.** Once Fig. 1 has shown what happens, later paragraphs do not announce it again ("This paper shows one way to break the cycle" was cut for this reason).
4. **State the scope.** The work is about mapping campaigns; optimisation is a different problem and is said to be out of scope.
5. **Describe the illustration in plain words.** Introduce Fig. 1 with its setup (what is fitted, what is set aside, what decides a measurement) before its outcome. Spell out acronyms at first use (GPR).
6. **Keep design and guarantee apart.** The hierarchical levels serve image formation and space filling; the guarantee rests only on the calibration draw. Say so wherever the levels appear.
7. **Prepare later ideas.** Plant the premise of an argument early (the guarantee is paid for in advance, before it is known whether the model is right) and develop it later.
8. **First person plural for the authors' own argument** ("we argue", "this paper shows"); impersonal constructions elsewhere.
9. **The abstract is rewritten last**, once the body is final.

## Points to keep factually exact

- With its own probabilities the GPR did not always violate the policy: it held in nearly every campaign with the noise fitted (too cautious), in as few as 69 % with the noise too low, and in none with the length scale too large.
- Acceptance sampling draws its sample at random from the lot; the calibration sample of each round is drawn the same way.
