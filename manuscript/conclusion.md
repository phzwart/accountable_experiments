# 7 Conclusion (draft 2)

An experiment that leaves sites unmeasured can be held to account. Each skip is posed as a claim about a site, recorded with its reason, and calibrated on a uniform draw from a level fixed in advance; the order fit, freeze, draw, calibrate, decide keeps each draw exchangeable with its level whatever came before. On four fully measured datasets the record matched the guarantees, with informed and random scores, natural-neighbour and Gaussian-process interpolation and three forms of guarantee. The model set the cost of a guarantee, and the certified claim decided what it bought: where measurements were taken, where wrong skips gathered and which sites were protected. The measured sites remained good data and recovered the kinetics of a simulated calcination as accurately as the full design. The claim therefore belongs to the scientific question and is best fixed with it, before the experiment. Label-conditional bounds that protect the sites of interest, optimisation through level sets, and scores that rank sites by the information they add to a process model are natural next steps. Each skipped site leaves the campaign with a reason, a claim and a bound, and the record allows every decision to be replayed.
