# 7 Conclusion (draft 2)

An experiment that leaves sites unmeasured can certify every action. A measurement is certified by the reason it was needed when the decision was made; a skip is certified by a claim about the site, calibrated on a uniform draw from a level fixed in advance; the order fit, freeze, draw, calibrate, decide keeps each draw exchangeable with its level whatever came before. On three fully measured datasets the record matched the guarantees, with informed and random scores, natural-neighbour and Gaussian-process interpolation and three forms of guarantee. The model set the cost of a guarantee; left uncertified, the same model misstated its error rate in either direction, and decisions priced on that statement paid for it. The certified claim decided what a guarantee bought: where measurements were taken, where wrong skips gathered and which sites were protected. The claim therefore belongs to the scientific question and enters the contract with it, before the experiment. Label-conditional bounds that protect the sites of interest, optimisation through level sets, scores that rank sites by the information they add to a process model, and deployment at autonomous instruments, beginning with a synthesis campaign, are natural next steps. Each skipped site leaves the campaign with a reason, a claim and a bound, and the record allows every decision to be replayed.
