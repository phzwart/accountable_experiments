# 7 Conclusion (draft 3)

An autonomous experiment that leaves sites unmeasured can be held to a contract fixed before it starts. The contract names the plan, the claims under which a site may be skipped, and a budget on wrong skips with its confidence. On each level of the plan one decision is taken, after a uniform draw from a level fixed before it, and a PAC bound on that decision, a share skipped wrongly below the budget at least as often as the stated confidence, is known before any of its sites is skipped; the order fit, freeze, draw, calibrate, decide keeps each draw exchangeable with its level whatever came before, so the bound holds for any model. The model steering a campaign cannot supply such a bound, since it chooses the data on which it would be judged; its statement about itself is checked only afterwards, against an audit or the full scan. On three fully measured datasets the record matched the guarantees, with informed and random scores and with natural-neighbour and Gaussian-process prediction. The model set the cost, and knowing the bound in advance was paid for in measurements, up to 41 % for a plan certified on all levels together. The certified claim decided what a guarantee bought, so the claim belongs to the scientific question and enters the contract with it. A held-out audit bounded the error of the finished map for any campaign, as the free R value does for a crystal structure. Deployment at autonomous instruments, beginning with a synthesis campaign, optimisation through level sets and a model of beam time are the next steps. Each skipped site leaves the campaign with a reason and a claim, each level with a PAC bound fixed before its decision, and the record allows every decision to be replayed.
